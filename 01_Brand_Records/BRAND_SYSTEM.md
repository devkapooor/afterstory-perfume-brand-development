## Visual and Copy Direction

- Keep the brand independent and understated rather than mainstream or mass-market.
- Prefer quiet, specific, emotionally recognizable moments. Leave space for the viewer to complete the story.
- Show evidence that something just happened. Absence can carry the story without showing people.
- Copy and imagery should complement each other, not repeat the same message. Vary the narrative; do not repeat "memories remain" in every post.
- Express premium quality through restraint, whitespace, typography, texture, and narrative, not gold or ornamentation.
- Build an incident, not a quote: place, small physical details, implied person or moment, then unresolved emotional residue.
- Show the aftermath rather than necessarily the event. Car scenes can use an empty passenger seat, scarf, coffee, and rain; cafe scenes can use two cups, one empty chair, a receipt, and late-evening light.

Reference caption:

> You had already left.
> But your scarf was still on the seat,
> the coffee was still warm,
> and the car still smelled like you.
> I sat there a little longer than I needed to.

The supplied Instagram visual guide remains the detailed authority for social scenes, photography settings, caption rules, and narrative sequence.

## Packaging Architecture

**8 mL:** 60 x 55 mm continuous wrap across four 15 mm panels. Front: brand, chapter, fragrance name, and accent. Story: vertical sentence. Product: Extrait de Parfum / 8 ML / 25% on one vertical axis. Scent: three-part profile. Use ivory, charcoal, vertical typography, strong negative space, restrained scent accent, no large illustration, gradients, or clutter.

**50 mL:** supplied bottle diameter 45 mm; 138 x 40 mm wrap with approximately 3.4 mm seam gap. Panels: 34.5 / 69 / 34.5 mm. Left: notes, website, handle, and SCENTS FOR WHAT REMAINS. Middle: brand, chapter, name, fine rule, story, Extrait de Parfum, 25% concentration, separator, and 50 ML. Right: scent-specific artwork only. Use a warm-ivory textured appearance, fine charcoal rules, subtly rounded corners, and the horizontal editorial 1:2:1 architecture.

Never enlarge the 8 ML design as a substitute for the 50 ML system. The 138 x 40 mm height supersedes both the earlier 138 x 45 mm wrap and the earlier 50 x 50 mm front-label proposal. Existing PDFs require adaptation to 40 mm height.
