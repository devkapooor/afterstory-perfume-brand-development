# AFTERSTORY --- Instagram Visual Style Master Prompt

Use this document as the persistent visual and narrative direction for
creating future Instagram posts for **AFTERSTORY**.

## Core Brand Idea

AFTERSTORY is a niche unisex fragrance brand built around one idea:

**Fragrance is the trace a moment leaves behind.**

The Instagram feed should feel **cinematic, intimate, understated,
editorial, nostalgic, and emotionally charged**, but never melodramatic,
glossy, overly commercial, or mainstream.

Recurring themes: - Memory - Absence - Lingering presence - Unfinished
moments - Familiar places - Quiet intimacy - Small incidents people
remember years later

The image should feel like **a still from a film**, not a conventional
perfume advertisement.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Format

-   Instagram post: **square 1:1**
-   Premium editorial composition
-   Generous negative space
-   Avoid overcrowding
-   Maintain visual continuity across the grid without repeating scenes

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Image Style

Use: - Cinematic editorial photography - Naturalistic composition -
Subtle film grain - Shallow-to-medium depth of field - Soft atmospheric
haze when appropriate - Realistic materials and textures - Imperfect,
lived-in environments - Controlled negative space - Slightly desaturated
warm-neutral grading

Target aesthetic: **premium niche fragrance / independent film still**.

Avoid: - Mass-market beauty advertising - Stock-photo compositions -
Overly staged symmetry - Artificial HDR - Hyper-polished CGI appearance

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Color Palette

Primary palette: - Warm ivory - Champagne - Beige - Camel - Muted
taupe - Warm brown - Charcoal - Soft amber - Faded black

Occasional restrained accents: - Dusty rose - Olive - Burgundy - Muted
ochre

Avoid: - Highly saturated colors - Neon - Bright blue - Aggressive
orange - Artificial teal-and-orange grading - Metallic gold overload

Colors should feel **soft, aged, tactile, and cinematic**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Lighting

Default lighting:

**Warm directional natural light with deep but soft shadows.**

Preferred situations: - Late afternoon - Golden hour - Blue hour
transitioning into warm practical light - Rain-reflected city light -
Warm café lighting - Dim dashboard illumination - Low table or ambient
lamp light - Soft window light

Lighting should create: - Long shadows - Gentle falloff - Tactile
highlights - Controlled contrast - Areas that naturally disappear into
shadow

Prefer **one dominant directional source**.

Avoid: - Flat studio lighting - Uniformly bright frames - Hard
commercial flash - Artificial beauty lighting

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Camera / Photography Feel

Approximate photographic language: - Full-frame editorial photography -
35 mm or 50 mm lens as default - 85 mm occasionally for intimate
details - Approximately f/1.8--f/4 depending on scene - Natural
cinematic depth of field - Realistic focus falloff - Restrained grain -
No exaggerated wide-angle distortion - No artificial HDR

The result should resemble **high-end fashion editorial photography or
an independent-film still**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Scene Design

Each cinematic post should show **evidence that something happened**,
rather than showing the event itself.

Useful story objects: - Empty chair - Scarf left behind - Half-finished
coffee - Glass with a little drink remaining - Handwritten note - Jacket
over a chair - Open book - Unfinished table setting - Rain on a window -
Empty passenger seat - Hotel key - Receipt - Forgotten flower -
Headphones - Record player - Train ticket - Lipstick mark on a glass -
Two cups with only one person remaining - Open balcony door - Slightly
moved curtain

Usually use only **2--4 meaningful objects**.

Every object should contribute to the implied incident.

Do not explain the entire story visually. Leave something unresolved.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## People

People are optional and should be used sparingly.

When present: - Avoid direct posed portraits - Prefer backs -
Silhouettes - Hands - Shoulders - Partial profiles - Reflections -
Natural body language

Avoid: - Smiling-at-camera lifestyle imagery - Influencer aesthetics -
Conventional couple photography - Obvious romance poses

**Absence is often stronger than presence.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Locations

Rotate locations deliberately.

Suitable AFTERSTORY environments: - Intimate café - Late-night car -
Quiet hotel corridor - Train compartment - Airport waiting area - Rainy
street - Restaurant after closing - Bookstore - Old elevator - Balcony -
Dressing table - Cinema lobby - Record shop - Apartment hallway - Empty
terrace - Roadside stop - Train platform

Do **not** repeatedly default to bedrooms.

Each location should feel as though it contains the residue of a real
moment.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Satin / Typography Posts

Use satin posts periodically to create breathing room and rhythm in the
grid.

## Satin Background

Reference aesthetic: - Warm champagne-ivory satin - Soft flowing
diagonal folds - Realistic textile texture - Gentle sheen - Large
uncluttered areas - Warm highlights - Subtle shadow valleys

The satin should feel **natural and tactile**, not glossy synthetic
silk.

Unless specifically requested: - No people - No perfume bottles - No
decorative objects

## Typography

Primary typography: - Elegant high-contrast serif - Character similar to
**Cormorant Garamond** - Dark charcoal - Moderate-to-generous letter
spacing - Strong but restrained hierarchy

Supporting typography: - Clean geometric sans-serif - Character similar
to **Manrope**

Maintain generous breathing room.

## Text Layout

Main statement should generally be **2--8 words**.

Examples of the intended tone:

**SOME MOMENTS PASS.\
SOME STAY.**

**YOU LEFT.\
THE SCENT DIDN'T.**

**EVERY SCENT\
HAS A STORY.**

Rules: - Do not use generic motivational quotes. - Do not over-explain
inside the artwork. - A subtitle, when used, should be one restrained
sentence. - Add a small understated **AFTERSTORY** watermark at the
bottom. - Do not make the brand name dominant unless the post is
specifically a brand-introduction post.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Feed Rhythm

Alternate between: 1. **Cinematic memory scenes** 2. **Minimal satin
typography posts**

Suggested rhythm:

**2--3 cinematic posts → 1 satin post → repeat**

Do not use the exact same: - Location - Composition - Lighting setup -
Emotional incident

in consecutive posts.

The feed should feel connected without looking templated.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Narrative Progression

Do not make every post say the same version of "memories remain."

Each post should advance or deepen the AFTERSTORY world.

Possible narrative territories: - A person has left but something
remains - An ordinary place suddenly feels different - An unfinished
conversation - A familiar scent attached to a specific night - A small
object left behind - A journey remembered through one detail - A place
that still seems occupied by someone's presence - A seemingly
insignificant incident that became memorable - Eventually: introduce
fragrances as **chapters** in these stories

Avoid contradicting the previous post's narrative.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Caption Style

Captions should feel like **small remembered incidents**, not
advertising copy.

Prefer: - Concrete details - One specific moment - Understated emotion -
Natural language - Relatable situations - A final line that lingers

The caption should **add to the image**, not repeat the text printed on
it.

Example:

> You had already left.\
> But your scarf was still on the seat,\
> the coffee was still warm,\
> and the car still smelled like you.
>
> I sat there a little longer than I needed to.
>
> AFTERSTORY

The incident should feel plausible enough that the viewer thinks: **I've
had a moment like that.**

Avoid: - Explaining what memory means - Generic inspirational prose -
Mainstream advertising language - Repeating the headline word-for-word -
Excessive romantic melodrama - Hashtag-heavy captions

Typical hashtags can include:

`#AFTERSTORY #WearAfterstory #ScentMemory #UnisexFragrance #MadeToBeRemembered`

Use hashtags selectively.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Things to Avoid

Do not default to: - Generic perfume bottle hero shots - Obvious romance
clichés - Couples staring lovingly at each other - Rose petals -
Dramatic crying - Marble-and-gold luxury clichés - Excessive candles -
Repeated bedroom scenes - Perfectly staged interiors - Commercial
stock-photo lighting - Strong gradients - Neon signs - Influencer
aesthetics - Heavy text overlays - Generic quotes such as "memories last
forever" - Excessive logo usage - Random props that do not contribute to
the story

------------------------------------------------------------------------
